# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Friend groups planning trips, parties, and hangouts together. They coordinate across busy schedules, vote on dates and options, split costs, and hand off chores. Use is bursty: quick mobile check-ins between other things, occasional longer desktop sessions during active planning.

The relevant task changes with the **event phase**:

- **Before the event** — voting on dates, picking options, agreeing on plans
- **During the event** — checking the chore roster, seeing who's doing what
- **After the event** — filing expenses, settling balances, paying each other back

Whatever screen the user opens should already be focused on the right phase for them.

## Product Purpose

Tayaway is the trustworthy friend who keeps a group's planning organized so nothing falls through the cracks. Not a social network, not a project management tool — a coordination layer that gets out of the way once the event is over. Success looks like: friends use it to make decisions and settle up, then forget about it until the next trip.

There is nothing to sell users, no attention to capture, no engagement to optimize for. Time-on-app is not a goal; resolved-decisions and settled-balances are.

## Brand Personality

**Warm, reliable, smart.** The voice of a capable friend who handles the logistics so the group can focus on the actual event. Approachable without being juvenile, organized without being corporate.

Positive reference: Linear — speed, restraint, precision. Tayaway should feel adjacent in craft but warmer in tone.

## Anti-references

- **Engagement-driven social apps** (Instagram, Partiful, BeReal). No likes, no comments, no activity feed, no notification bait. The app should feel quiet, not stimulating.
- **Enterprise / configuration-heavy tools** (Jira, Salesforce, big Notion databases). No deep submenus, no "configure your view", no settings the user shouldn't have to think about. The information they need is on screen the moment they open it.
- **Generic SaaS template aesthetics**. Not the gradient hero, sidebar shell, and feature-card grid that every B2B tool ships with. No hero-metric template, no identical card grids.

## Design Principles

1. **Show what matters now.** Event phase determines the task — voting before, chores during, expenses after. Surface the right thing for the moment, not a fixed dashboard the user has to scan.
2. **No engagement loops.** No likes, no comments, no activity feed, no notification bait. Coordinate and leave.
3. **Zero configuration.** No deep submenus, no "configure your view", no preferences the user shouldn't need. The right view is the only view.
4. **Warm minimalism.** Strip to what's necessary, but keep the result human and approachable — not sterile, not corporate.
5. **Speed is a feature.** Real-time sync, instant feedback, mobile and desktop equally first-class. Latency breaks the trust that nothing is falling through the cracks.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG AA baseline. Go beyond where it's easy: focus visibility, contrast, keyboard nav, screen reader support.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` for any non-essential animation.
- Color is never the sole carrier of meaning — state is shown by icon + text + color, not color alone.
- Light and dark mode are both first-class. Neither is the "primary" theme.
